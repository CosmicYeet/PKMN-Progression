The Loop

STEP 01

Crack the set

Everyone opens the same 18 packs of the newest set in the sim — ideally live together.

STEP 02

Grow your pool

Add every card you pulled to your personal cumulative collection, and log it on the Card Pools page. It only ever grows.

STEP 03

Rebuild the deck

Construct a fresh 60-card deck from your entire pool, not just the new set. Full rebuilds allowed every session.

STEP 04

Battle

Run the night's Swiss rounds in TTS. Log results to the Standings board, then queue up the next set.

02

The Golden Rule

Read this one first

Basic Energy is free. Special Energy is pulled.

A 60-card deck needs 12–20 Energy. If you could only run the Energy you opened, decks would be unplayable for the first month. So basic Energy sits outside the pool entirely — and in TTS you just spawn it from the card database. Everything else you play must come from cards you actually pulled.

// UNLIMITED
Basic EnergyGrass, Fire, Water, Lightning, Psychic, Fighting, Metal, Darkness. Add as many as you like.
// PULL IT
Special EnergyDouble Colorless, Rainbow, and any other named Energy count toward your pool like any card.

03

Deckbuilding

  • Deck sizeExactly 60 cards. Free basic Energy conveniently fills the gap when your early pool is thin.
  • Copy limitMax 4 of any card (basic Energy exempt). You run only the copies you actually pulled.
  • BasicsAt least one Basic Pokémon — realistically several. Thin Basic counts mean brutal mulligans.
  • LegalityYour deck is audited against your logged pulls on the Card Pools page. If a card isn't in your pool, it can't be in your deck.
  • RebuildFull teardown allowed every session from your whole cumulative pool.
  • BansStart with none. The Commissioner adds restrictions only if something dominates.
04

Battle Rules

  • As printedPlay cards by their era's text — in Base–Neo, Weakness doubles damage (×2, not +30) and Trainers have no once-per-turn limit.
  • Turn 1House rule: the player going first doesn't draw and can't attack on their first turn.
  • Win byTake all 6 Prizes, leave the opponent with no Pokémon in play, or deck them out at the start of their turn.
  • MatchBest of three per Swiss pairing. The first player to win 2 games wins the match.

06

The Season

Swiss pairings, points that carry

With a full table a round-robin is too many games. Each night, pair players by current record (Swiss) and run 3 rounds so games stay competitive. Points accumulate across the entire progression into the running Standings board.

Match points reward both winning and taking games: a 2–0 win earns 3, a 2–1 win earns 2, a 1–2 loss earns 1, and a 0–2 loss earns 0. Untimed Pokémon effectively never ties, so there is no draw score. Odd turnout? One player takes a bye, worth 1.5 points. If you ever play on a clock, break a timed-out game on prizes taken, then a sudden-death prize.

Era Championships

At the close of each era on the Season Track, the top two on points play a Bo3 final for that era's title. Reset nothing; points roll onward. Win the most era titles and you take the overall crown when the progression ends.

07

Tabletop Simulator Setup

  1. In the sim, select the newest set, set quantity to 18 (plus any Special sets due this session at their pack count), and Auto Open. Then hit Export → TTS.
  2. In Tabletop Simulator, load a maintained Pokémon TCG mod — Pepperoni's table is the actively-updated one with the full card database and a decklist-import tablet.
  3. Keep one saved object as your personal collection. Each session, add the packs you just exported to it — that's your growing pool.
  4. Build your 60-card deck from your collection, spawning unlimited basic Energy straight from the mod's database.
  5. Sit in your player color, set up, and battle. Save your deck as an object so next session you can tweak instead of rebuild from scratch.

Lighter alternatives if TTS is fussy for someone: the sim's own on-site Play table, or the browser-based ptcgsim.online.
